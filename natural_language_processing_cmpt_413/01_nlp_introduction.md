# NLP introduction

NLP turns ambiguous, evolving language into useful predictions and actions through representation, composition, and learning.

## The central problem

Language communicates things, actions, and abstract concepts between a speaker and a listener. It is ambiguous, context-dependent, and constantly changing. NLP builds useful systems that process this language: translation, classification, question answering, text generation, coding assistance, and agents that act on instructions. Language can also condition outputs in another modality, as in text-to-image generation.

Rare words and combinations create a long tail, so even large datasets leave gaps.

A useful model needs three things:

1. **Representation:** turn words or sentences into a computational form.
2. **Composition:** combine units while preserving information relevant to the task.
3. **Prediction:** map the representation to a label, output sequence, or action.
