# NLP introduction and course setup

NLP turns ambiguous, evolving language into useful predictions and actions through representation, composition, and learning.

## The central problem

Language communicates things, actions, and abstract concepts between a speaker and a listener. It is ambiguous, context-dependent, and constantly changing. NLP builds useful systems that process this language: translation, classification, question answering, text generation, coding assistance, and agents that act on instructions. Language can also condition outputs in another modality, as in text-to-image generation.

Rare words and combinations create a long tail, so even large datasets leave gaps.

A useful model needs three things:

1. **Representation:** turn words or sentences into a computational form.
2. **Composition:** combine units while preserving information relevant to the task.
3. **Prediction:** map the representation to a label, output sequence, or action.

## Course expectations

Expected background includes Python, calculus, linear algebra, probability, and machine learning. Multivariable derivatives are particularly important. Programming uses NumPy and PyTorch. Lectures focus on concepts; programming tools require independent practice, and optional tutorials support background review.

Programming assignments are group work for teams of two or three, with each member contributing and their contributions described. Conceptual assignments are individual work. Written answers should be clear and succinct. Conceptual answers and reports are submitted through Gradescope; programming submissions use Coursys.

The class project is a small research project, such as reimplementing a recent NLP paper or experimentally comparing several methods, carried out by a team of two or three.

LLM assistance requires understanding the submitted work. When an LLM helps develop an answer, explain the initial attempt, what it corrected, why those corrections were needed, and what was learned. The student remains responsible for accuracy, attribution, and the submitted explanation.
